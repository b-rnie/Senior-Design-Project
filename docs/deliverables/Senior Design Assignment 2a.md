Customer personas & empathy maps

Profile representations of primary, secondary, and administrative end-users detailing goals, pain points, behaviors, and operating environments

**Primary End-Users: Adopters/Fosters B2C**

- **Goals:** ****Their goal in PetLoop is to find a forever pet or a pet to temporarily foster and care for! Through the engaging videos they hope to understand the pets and their personalities to ensure a better fit for the person’s lifestyle and encourage less rehoming which is stressful for the primary user and pet.

- **Pain Points:** Currently with most static image adoption apps or sites, primary users cannot experience understanding what an animal will act or how they will behave with others in real interactions. Through a video first experience rather than image first, a user can accurately be conveyed the animal’s personality reducing decision paralysis that may lead a user to reconsider rehoming an animal.

- **Behaviors:** ****The primary user demographic can range from young adults or older that are looking to own an animal. Since this demographic range typically uses many apps like TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and more, their behaviors are more oriented towards fast paced informational content to consume which rely on swiped based mechanics and story visual storytelling media.

- **Operating Environment:** ****iOS or Android Apps for mobile devices. These users will use the app as a way to pass time like other social media platforms when in search for an animal or to share with others.

 **Secondary End-Users: Shelter Staff B2B**

- **Goals:** Their goal in PetLoop is to process the intake process of receiving an animal and putting them up through the adoption/foster process as soon as possible with assisted descriptions and tagging in a central location. This is so they can focus more time on animal care rather than creating profiles, which is time consuming and gets a pet to their forever home sooner with an easier intake process.

- **Pain Points:** Shelter staff are limited in staff and have limited time to care for pets as is but also need to work with outdated shelter software which requires writing creative biographies by hand and writing all the traits of said animals for dozens of animals.

- **Behaviors:** Shelter staff are constantly multi-tasking in the roles they perform from posting animals for rehoming, caring for animals, and taking in questions. They are capable of the training in old software established but when it comes to creating more engaging ways to connect with the community consistently it can be a complete overtake in the current process which they don’t have time for.  

- **Operating Environment:** Since it is a fast-paced environment with lots to do, users for PetLoop will typically already have an tablet or mobile device on hand or within the shelter for communicating with other staff, uploading photos of animals in their care, taking notes for animals, and more.

**Administrative End-Users: Shelter Director or Rescue** **Lead B2B**

- **Goals:** Their goal with PetLoop is to maximize the overall adoption or fostering rates among their community and shelter. By reducing the length of stay for an animal in their care,  they can take in more animals and even provide more quality focused care to the animals they have. This can also free up time for their staff to organize more branding through PetLoop.

- **Pain Points:** Directors struggle with staff turnover which takes time to retrain new volunteers or staff when the volume of animals in their care and length of their stay continue to increase. Lack of centralized marketing platform makes it harder to market to people willing to adopt when their staff manages between adoption software and social media platform. 

- **Behaviors:** Data driven and organizational behaviors where the Administrator wants to monitor their platform analytics, accounts and permissioning for the staff. They also should be able to review any AI generated content before publication to their customers.

- **Operating Environment:** Office based environment where they manage emails, business, and more. PetLoop could provide a web dashboard so that users could use this app on their computers within the office. However, they also would have access to mobile devices like secondary users.

User Journey & interaction scenarios

Narrative scenarios tracing key stakeholder touchpoints from discovery through core system usage and resolution**.**

**Scenario 1:** **Finding a Home for a Pet**

**Persona:** Dan, an individual pet owner

**Goal:** Find a safe and responsible new home for his cat before moving.

**Challenges:** Dan is unsure where to post his pet and wants to feel confident that she will go to a responsible home.

**Environment:** Mainly uses his phone at home and has limited time to find a new home for his pet.

Dan is preparing to move into a rental that does not allow pets and needs to find a new home for his cat, Luna. A coworker tells him about PetLoop, so he downloads the app and creates an account. He creates a profile for Luna with information about her personality and habits and adds photos and a short video to help potential adopters get to know her. Once Luna's profile is posted, people using PetLoop can discover her while browsing. Dan begins receiving interest from potential adopters and reviews the responses. After talking with someone who seems like a good match, he arranges to meet them. Once Dan finds a home, he feels comfortable with, he updates Luna's profile as rehomed, so she is no longer shown as available.

**User Journey**

- **Discovery:** Dan hears about PetLoop from a coworker while looking for a way to rehome Luna.

- **Onboarding:** He creates an account and begins setting up Luna's profile.

- **Core Use:** He adds information, photos, and a short video and publishes the profile.

- **Action:** He receives interest and communicates with potential adopters.

- **Resolution:** Luna finds a new home, and Dan updates her status as rehomed.

**Scenario 2:** **Looking for a Pet to Adopt** 

**Persona**: Maya, a college student interested in adopting or fostering

**Goal**: Find a pet that fits her living situation, schedule, and lifestyle.

**Challenges**: Looking through several adoption websites can take time, and pictures alone may not give her a good idea of a pet's personality.

**Environment**: Mainly uses her phone between classes and during her free time.

Maya has been thinking about adopting or fostering a pet but is not sure where to start. She sees a PetLoop pet video shared on social media and decides to try the app. After creating an account, she begins browsing short videos of pets looking for homes. As Maya browses, she opens the profiles of pets that catch her attention to learn more about them. She saves a few that she wants to consider and returns to the app later to continue looking. Eventually, Maya finds a dog that seems like a good fit and expresses interest through PetLoop. The shelter receives her request and follows up with information about the next steps. After meeting the dog and completing the shelter's process, Maya moves forward with fostering the pet.

**User Journey**

-  **Discovery**: Maya sees a PetLoop pet video shared on social media.

- **Onboarding**: She creates an account and provides basic information about what she is looking for.

- **Core Use**: She browses videos, views pet profiles, and saves pets that interest her.

- **Action**: She expresses interest in a dog that seems like a good match.

- **Resolution**: The shelter follows up, and Maya moves forward with the fostering process.

**Scenario 3: Shelter or Rescue Listing a Pet**

**Persona:** Carlos, a staff member at a local animal rescue

**Goal:** Get animals listed quickly so they can reach potential adopters and fosters.

Challenges: Shelter staff have limited time, and creating and managing many pet listings can take time away from other responsibilities.

**Environment**: Uses a phone, tablet, or computer while working at the rescue**.**

A new dog arrives at Carlos's rescue and is ready to begin looking for a foster or permanent home. During the workday, Carlos opens Pet Loop and creates a profile for the dog. He adds the information the rescue knows about the animal along with photos and a short video.

Carlos reviews the information before publishing the profile to make sure it is accurate. Once published, users can discover the dog while browsing Pet Loop. Over the next several days, Carlos receives interest from potential adopters and fosters and follows up with them. After the rescue completes its normal placement process and the dog finds a home, Carlos updates the pet's status so users know the animal is no longer available.

**User Journey**

- **Discovery:** Carlos learns about Pet Loop as another way for the rescue to reach potential adopters and fosters**.**

-  **Onboarding:** He creates the rescue account and sets up its basic information.

- **Core Use:** He creates a pet profile and adds information, photos, and video**.**

- **Action:** He reviews incoming interest and follows up with potential adopters or fosters.

- **Resolution:** The pet finds a home, and Carlos updates its status.

**Scenario 4:** **Handling a Reported Issue**

**Persona:** Sam, a PetLoop administrator

**Goal:** Keep PetLoop's listings accurate and respond to reported problems**.**

**Challenges:** Misleading or inappropriate listings can affect trust in the platform, so reports need to be reviewed and handled properly.

**Environment:** Mainly uses an administrative dashboard on a computer**.**

Sam checks Pet Loop's administrative dashboard and sees that a user has reported a pet listing because some of the information appears misleading. Sam opens the report, reviews the listing, and looks at the reason it was reported. After reviewing the information, Sam determines that part of the listing needs to be corrected. The person who created the listing is asked to update the information. Once the correction is made, Sam reviews the listing again and closes the report. If a listing clearly violates Pet Loop's guidelines, Sam can take further action when necessary. This process helps keep the information on Pet Loop more accurate and trustworthy.

**User Journey**

- **Discovery:** Sam receives a new report through the administrative system.

- **Onboarding:** He reviews the report and the reason it was submitted.

- **Core Use:** He reviews the reported listing and relevant information.

- **Action:** He determines what action is needed and addresses the problem.

- **Resolution:** The issue is corrected or otherwise handled, and the report is closed.

Ethical & legal compliance risk assessment

**Software Engineering Ethics and Social Implications**

While building this application, the ACM Code of Ethics must be considered to ensure that it is built with the guidelines in mind and that the application will not harm the health, safety, or welfare of the public, more specifically the users of the app. Since PetLoop is an app that manages dog adoption, it is important that all information displayed pertaining to pets is honest and accurate. Any information provided by shelters or organizations should be truthful and not mislead a user’s decision or misrepresent a dog’s health condition. There must be clear guidelines for the process of adopting a pet through the app, allowing users enough time and details to make a knowledgeable decision. It must be ensured that users are not pressured by the app or the pet organizations, to quickly adopt a pet through manipulative means.

**Data Privacy Compliance**

In this current day and age, personal information is highly sought after from big companies and often collected without the user’s consent. From collecting personal information such as birthdates, emails, addresses, scrolling habits and visited websites, companies use this information to better advertise and sell their products. Users should still have the right to their own online identity- and to choose what information they want to share, and what remains private. With PetLoop, we would gather relevant information such as name, address, personal habits, details of their living situation, and scrolling habits. These factors would help our algorithms learn which pets are more suitable for the user, personalizing their feed to best optimize potential candidates. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) emphasizes data minimalization, asking explicit consent for what data the user chooses to share, and only collecting what is necessary. Upon account creation or in the section of a user’s profile, we would inform the user of both essential information needed to ensure pets go to an appropriate environment as well as nonessential information that is aimed more to improve preferences. Along with the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) which allows a user to request deletion of their data from a platform, our users can choose to delete any optional information from their profile down the line. 

**Accessibility Standards Audit**

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 operates around four principles that allow for diverse ways for media to be consumed so that it is accessible to every user. The first principle is **Perceivable**, in which users should be able to comprehend the media given to them. Since PetLoop is a series of videos on the user's feed, it would provide visual output, audio, and text transcriptions to allow a variety of ways to receive the information. The second principle is **Operable.** For the ease of the user, they would be able to use the built-in keyboard with its audio input ability included (relative to their device they are using) for searching, navigating, and general input usage. The UI would incorporate easy ways to identify how to navigate the app to show their feed, profile, search bar, and other pages of the app. The third principle is **Understandable**, for presenting information in an easy, understandable format. If a user wants to learn more information on a pet from a video they like, PetLoop would have an easy process (such as a swiping gesture or clicking on a profile) that would lead the user to obtain more information such as location, age, disposition, etc. of that pet with full transparency. The last principle is **Robust**, ensuring compatibility. PetLoop would support platforms like iOS and android, with a website across different web browsers as well. Additionally, users would be able to use their native device accessibility utilities (such as assistive touch on iOS) on PetLoop.  

**Intellectual Property and Licensing Considerations**

Since PetLoop would include an AI tool to sift through data and create personal algorithms to the user, we would need datasets that deal with the user’s behavior, patterns, etc. With the matter of intellectual property (IP) and licensing, PetLoop would primarily rely on datasets that are free to use, meaning we would look for datasets with licenses that are CC0, or CC-By. For any information that is under a CC-By license, we would include citation for the origin. As far as using frameworks, IDE, and third party-tools, using open source or permissive open-source software would be ideal for PetLoop. This allows us to use tools to create our app as well as monetize it. PetLoop would comply with any legal bindings that come with using said tool, such as attribution.
